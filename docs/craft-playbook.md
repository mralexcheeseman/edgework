# Craft playbook

Techniques borrowed from the writers the Wire follows, plus Cole Schafer for personality. Taken from recent full pieces read on 21 October 2026. We borrow **moves**, never sentences: nothing here is copied text, and the Desk is told never to imitate a named writer or reuse their phrasing. Everything is filtered through Alex's own voice (see `voice.md`).

## What each writer does well

| Writer | The move worth borrowing | Example of the move (described, not quoted) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Seth Godin** | One idea, under 200 words. Opens on a concrete, slightly odd fact; ends on a short instruction or verdict. No hedging, no sources section. | Two people who chose depth over metrics, then the turn: "we are the circumstances", then "start today". |
| **Morgan Housel** | Historical anecdote with exact numbers, widened to a universal human behaviour, then a personal admission ("Here's where I've landed"), then a reframe that flips the reader's judgement. | Infant mortality in the 18th century leads to spoiled children, and the turn: the spoiling *was* the goal. |
| **Ryan Holiday** | Numbers as rhythm in the opening (weeks, days, hours, each on its own line). Personal stakes before the lesson. | Counting years of study in units to make time physical. |
| **Nicholas Carr** | Quote the other side generously, then let dry understatement do the damage. Ends with a short, cold sting. | A columnist's elaborate phone-habit ritual, gently mocked, ending on a five-word verdict about who serves whom. |
| **Tom Goodwin** | Step back from the hype; name the two real options as a numbered pair; wry asides in brackets. Coins a frame (eras, "the ultimate finger bot"). | Agents as a patch over badly built software, with a deadpan line about logging into a lightbulb. |
| **Zoe Scaman** | News hook staged as a scene (who walked on stage wearing what), then a bracketed jab that exposes the money. Future told through one named person's day. | A cute AI mascot, then the reveal that it takes a commission. |
| **April Dunford** | Opens on the exact question a client asked; answers in numbered, practical steps; plain and warm. | "Our positioning will be out of date in two weeks. How do we handle that?" |
| **Ann Handley** | Outside versus inside: what someone looks like versus what they feel. A running gag threaded through the piece. Ends on a three-line landing. | The calm business traveller in 1A versus the sweating one, carried through a piece about search. |
| **Ben Thompson** | Explicit structure: names the framework, numbers the reasons ("First… Second… Third"), discloses his own conflict plainly. | Analysis that tells the reader where it's going and why. |
| **Cole Schafer** (personality) | Sensory, specific detail (brand names, food, place, temperature). Confession over authority. Repetition as rhythm ("After that, it was… After that, it was…"). Daily pages of under 100 words that turn once and stop. | Spending ideas recklessly, compared to something reckless and specific; rest the way you work. |

## The Edgework flourish rules

The personality layer, applied lightly. A draft should use **two or three** of these, never all of them:

1. **One sensory, physical detail** the reader can see, hear, taste or touch (Schafer, Alex). Not "a coffee shop": "the third flat white of the morning".
2. **One outrageous but exact simile**, used once (Schafer). It must be specific and slightly too much.
3. **A bracketed aside** that tells the truth the sentence was too polite to say (Scaman, Goodwin).
4. **Repetition as rhythm**: the same opening words three times, then break the pattern (Schafer, Holiday).
5. **A confession**: one sentence admitting Alex got this wrong too, or does it himself (Schafer, Housel).
6. **The dry understatement** instead of outrage (Carr).

## Recipes by format

**Hey Rube** (≤25 words; Godin and Schafer's daily pages)
One claim with a twist, built on a concrete noun. Reads like something you'd hear at a bar and repeat at work. No setup, no hedge.

**Dispatch** (250 to 400 words; Scaman, Carr, Housel)
1. A scene from the news or Alex's life, in the present tense (two to four sentences).
2. The jab: what's really going on, often in brackets.
3. Alex's position, stated in a two-beat reframe.
4. One specific piece of evidence from the material, quoted exactly and credited.
5. What it means for the reader this week.
6. A callback to the opening scene, landing on the pull line.

**Routine** (60 to 90 seconds, 150 to 220 words; Holiday, Schafer, Alex's essays)
Written for the ear, one sentence per line.
1. A cold open, second person, present tense: "It's 2am…"
2. Three short lines of escalation.
3. The turn: one line that flips it.
4. The idea in plain words, with one image.
5. A callback to the opening, then the line people repeat.

**Expense report** (Goodwin's wit, Scaman's jab)
Four to six line items, each a real, recognisable expense of the mistake, with a wry cost. The total is the verdict.

**Essay** (800 to 1,200 words; Housel, Thompson, Alex's long form)
1. A historical or personal anecdote with exact detail.
2. The consensus, stated fairly.
3. The counter-thesis, in a two-beat reframe.
4. The mechanism, explained once, with a named thinker's frame used in one line.
5. Evidence from the material.
6. The strongest objection, answered.
7. What to do on Monday.
8. A callback ending.

## The editor's checklist

The second pass scores the draft on each point and rewrites anything that fails:

- **Opening:** starts in a scene or a concrete fact, with no throat-clearing.
- **Enemy:** names the false belief being argued against.
- **Stakes:** why it matters now, and to whom.
- **Line:** has a sentence people would repeat, and it is the pull.
- **Voice:** sounds like Alex (see `voice.md`), not like the writer of the source piece.
- **Flourish:** two or three of the flourish rules, not more.
- **Specificity:** at least one exact detail (a number, a name, a place, an object).
- **Ending:** calls back to the opening, or lands on a short verdict.
- **Banned:** no em dashes, no "Not X, but Y", no filler words, no stacked questions, no hype, no US spelling.
- **Truth:** every quote appears in the material word for word, and every figure is in the material or clearly marked as Alex's view.
